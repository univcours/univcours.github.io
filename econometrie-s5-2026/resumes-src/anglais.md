# Anglais — Units 1 à 4
*Fiche de révision — Licence Économétrie S5 | 4 documents de cours étudiés*

## Ce qu'il faut savoir
Les quatre unités disponibles travaillent la compréhension de courts textes, la conversation, le vocabulaire et les règles de grammaire nécessaires pour se présenter, décrire sa famille, parler de ses habitudes et de son logement.

## Unit 1 — Introducing yourself
### 1. Greetings and introductions
| Situation | Useful expressions |
|---|---|
| Informal meeting | Hello! / Hi! / Nice to meet you. |
| Introducing yourself | My name is Amina. / I'm a student. / I'm from Morocco. |
| Asking a name | What's your name? / What's yours? |
| Introducing someone | This is my friend Sara. / I'd like to introduce you to Sara. |
| Formal setting | I would like to introduce myself. / It's a pleasure to meet you. |
| Replying politely | Nice to meet you too. / Likewise. / And you? |

The course dialogue features **Amina from Morocco** and **Moussa from Mali**. Reading comprehension questions ask about Morocco's and Mali's location, cities and cultural features.

**Countries and nationalities:** Morocco → Moroccan; France → French; Italy → Italian; Japan → Japanese; Canada → Canadian; Spain → Spanish. A country is a noun (« Morocco »), whereas the nationality is generally an adjective (« Moroccan »).

Nationality endings illustrated in the course include **-ish** (British, Spanish), **-ian** (Italian, Canadian), **-ese** (Japanese, Portuguese), **-i** (Pakistani, Iraqi), and **-an** (Moroccan, American).

### 2. Grammar — Verb TO BE (present simple)
| Subject | Affirmative | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | I am / I'm | I am not / I'm not | Am I...? |
| You | You are / You're | You aren't | Are you...? |
| He / She / It | He is / He's | He isn't | Is he...? |
| We / They | We are / They're | They aren't | Are they...? |

Uses: identity (She is a student), origin (They are Moroccan), age (He is 20), present state (I am tired), location (Are they in class?).

**Short answers:** Are you a student? → Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. (Don't answer *Yes, I am a student* every time if the context is already clear.)

### 3. Model introduction
"Hello, my name is Lina. I am from Morocco. I am a university student. I study economics. Nice to meet you!"

### Practice
1. Complete: "My friend ___ from Egypt." → **is**.
2. "___ you a student?" → **Are**.
3. "They ___ at the university today." → **are**.
4. Rewrite formally: "Hi, I'm Sue." → "I'd like to introduce myself. My name is Sue."

## Unit 2 — Family members
### 1. Vocabulary: immediate and extended family
- **Parents:** mother (mom), father (dad).
- **Children and siblings:** son, daughter, brother, sister; twin brother/sister.
- **Spouse:** husband or wife.
- **Extended family:** grandmother, grandfather, aunt, uncle, cousin, niece, nephew, great-grandparents.
- **By marriage:** mother-in-law, father-in-law, sister-in-law, brother-in-law.
- **Other:** stepmother/stepfather, half-brother/half-sister.

**Careful:** My mother's sister is my **aunt**. My sister's son is my **nephew**. My aunt's daughter is my **cousin**.

The reading passage presents **Monica**, her family members and their occupations. The comprehension questions ask about names, professions, grandparents and cousins.

### 2. Descriptive adjectives
Adjectives describe appearance or character:
- Physical: tall, short, beautiful, brown-haired, young, old.
- Character: kind, reliable, optimistic, generous, shy, rude, hardworking, cheerful, easygoing.

In English, descriptive adjectives typically appear **before a noun** ("a kind person") or **after a linking verb** ("She is kind"). Avoid French-style order in "a beautiful house."

### 3. Possessive adjectives
| Person | Adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | my | My sister is a student. |
| you | your | Your father is kind. |
| he | his | His name is Omar. |
| she | her | Her job is interesting. |
| it | its | The dog has its toy. |
| we | our | Our family is big. |
| they | their | Their children are happy. |

Always put the possessive adjective **before a noun**: *their house*, not *theirs house*.

### 4. Singular and plural nouns
- Usually add **-s**: book → books.
- After -s, -sh, -ch, -x often **-es**: bus → buses; box → boxes.
- Consonant + -y → **-ies**: city → cities, baby → babies.
- Some -f/-fe forms → **-ves**: leaf → leaves, knife → knives.
- Irregular forms must be learned: child → children, man → men, person → people, mouse → mice, tooth → teeth.
- Some are unchanged: sheep → sheep, deer → deer.

**Writing task:** describe 4–6 sentences about family appearance and personality, then a paragraph of 5–7 sentences with family members, jobs, hobbies and character.

## Unit 3 — Daily routines
### 1. Vocabulary of daily life
- wake up, get up, brush my teeth, have a shower, have breakfast;
- leave home, go to work/university, have a meeting, have lunch;
- leave work, do the shopping, do the housework, do the ironing;
- stay in, watch TV, read, go to bed, fall asleep, take a nap, have a lie-in.

**Common collocations:** *do* the shopping, *brush* my teeth, *take* a nap, *fall* asleep, *play* cards, *have* a shower.

### 2. Present Simple
Use present simple for routines, permanent facts and general truths:
- I/you/we/they **work**. He/she/it **works**.
- I/you/we/they **do not (don't) work**. She **does not (doesn't) work**.
- **Do** you study English? / **Does** she study English?
- Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't.

**Third-person -s:** study → studies; go → goes; watch → watches. After does/doesn't, use the **base verb**, not another -s: "She doesn't like football."

### 3. Adverbs of frequency
**Always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely/seldom, never** indicate how regularly something occurs. The course includes exercises placing them in sentences.

- **Before most main verbs:** I **usually get up** at 7:00.
- **After the verb BE:** She **is often** at home.
- With auxiliaries: I **don't usually go** out on weekdays.

### 4. WH questions
**What, when, where, who, why, how, how often, what time**.

Examples:
- What time do you wake up?
- Where do you work?
- How often do you use the internet?
- Does she have a shower in the morning?

### Model paragraph
"I usually get up at 7 a.m. I brush my teeth and have breakfast. I go to university in the morning. I sometimes study with friends in the afternoon. I often read at night, and I usually go to bed at 11 p.m."

## Unit 4 — Home and living
### 1. Rooms and furniture
- Rooms: living room, kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, dining room, balcony, yard.
- Furniture/appliances: sofa, armchair, table, chair, bed, bookcase, dresser, lamp, refrigerator, stove, curtains, rug, elevator.

The dialogues describe an apartment near the university, its rooms, whether it has an elevator or a view, and a second apartment needing furniture.

### 2. Grammar — There is / There are
| Meaning | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | There is a sofa. | There are two chairs. |
| Negative | There isn't a table. | There aren't any rugs. |
| Question | Is there a balcony? | Are there any books? |

**There is** is used for one thing; **there are** for multiple. The course practises **some, any, no** and the short forms **there's, isn't, aren't**.

### 3. Prepositions of place
The unit introduces position words used to describe rooms: **in, on, under, behind, in front of, next to, between, above, opposite**. Use them to answer: "Where is the lamp?" → "It is next to the sofa."

### 4. Model description of a home
"I live in an apartment near the university. There is a large living room and a small kitchen. There are two bedrooms. There is a sofa next to the window. My desk is in my bedroom. There isn't a balcony, but there is a nice view."

**Speaking task:** describe your dream home: location, rooms, furniture and view. The reading exercise also asks about unusual hotel rooms such as the Roxbury and Bubble Hotel.

## Quick grammar table
| Point | Correct | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| TO BE | She **is** Moroccan. | She **are** Moroccan. |
| Possessive | This is **her** book. | This is **she** book. |
| Present simple | He **studies** English. | He **study** English. |
| Present simple negative | She **doesn't work** today. | She doesn't works today. |
| Adverb position | I **often read**. | I read often (less standard emphasis). |
| BE + frequency | We **are usually** early. | We usually are early (context-dependent). |
| There is/are | **There are** three rooms. | There is three rooms. |
| Plural | Two **children**. | Two childs. |

## Training: ten questions with answers
1. Introduce yourself in English. → "Hello, I'm …, I'm from …, and I study …"
2. "___ he from Mali?" → **Is**.
3. What is your father's brother? → **Uncle**.
4. Complete: "They have a house. It is ___ house." → **their**.
5. Give the plural of *city*. → **cities**.
6. "She (watch) TV every evening." → **watches**.
7. "He (not like) football." → **doesn't like**.
8. Put *usually*: "My friends are late." → "My friends **are usually** late."
9. "___ there a sofa?" → **Is**.
10. Describe a kitchen with two chairs. → "**There are two chairs** in the kitchen."
